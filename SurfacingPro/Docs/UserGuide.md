# SurfacingPro — user guide

SurfacingPro is a level-modelling mode for Unreal Engine 5.7. You build in the level with **primitives** (boxes and cylinders) and **patches** (bezier grids), put materials on them by projection - nothing is ever unwrapped - paint layers over them, keep repeated pieces as **prefabs** and **levels**, and what you built plays and ships as Nanite static meshes without a bake step you have to think about. Everything is metric and everything snaps to a grid.

This guide is written for the Blender key scheme, the default. The house scheme (W E R for move, turn, size) is at the end; every key can be changed in the settings.

---

## 1. Getting in

Enable the plugin (Edit → Plugins → SurfacingPro) and restart the editor. In the level editor's mode toolbar choose **SurfacingPro** - or double-click any of our geometry in the level, shipped meshes included. A new primitive or patch is always brought into view and near enough to work on: the camera slides, keeping its turn, only when the piece would be off screen or far off. The mode puts a **top strip** under the viewport's own toolbar - the selection kinds first, always at the top left (Auto, 1 2 3, and the box for Object), the family menus, the level you are in (its mark and name in a quiet blue, amber inside a prefab) with the **ghost** (open with the level around it) and the in / out arrows beside it, then the switches - and a **sidebar** against the left edge of the viewport (right, if you prefer, under Interface); **N** opens the **SidePanel** down the other edge.

The sidebar holds two things. At the top, the **pinned** buttons - **Primitive · Patch · Extrude · Cut · Weld · Delete** to start with - always in view. Under them a small arrow folds or unfolds the **shelf**: the buttons you added to the sidebar, grouped by family with a small gap between groups and no titles. In Edit UI, a button dragged off the sidebar and let go on nothing leaves it. The **Actions** window's family headings fold and unfold on a click, remembered. Right-click any button - on the sidebar, in a menu, in the **UVs** or the **Actions** window - for **Options...**, **Add to the Q menu**, **Add the whole menu to the Q menu**, **Add to sidebar** and **Pin**. Every button on the top strip - Auto, 1 2 3, Object, Snap, Meshes, Soft, the ghost, the arrows, Game - has the same lines on its right-click, and so do the X search's results, the windows in the Windows menu and Edit UI / Settings in Interface. Right-click a menu itself - a family on the top strip, a family in the viewport's right-click menu, a sidebar tab, a heading in Actions - to put the whole menu in the Q menu as one entry; **Q** opens it at the cursor. Right-clicked inside a menu, the menu stays open under the small one until you pick. **Actions** (Windows, or the Actions verb) is the window with every verb, always, in its families; it docks anywhere. The sidebar's width and length have grips along its edge and under it, faint until the cursor finds them; drag it narrow enough and it keeps only its tabs. Under **Edit UI** (a verb in View; its right-click holds the rest of the interface) the buttons move by drag alone - onto a button takes its place, before or after by the half you drop on - every tab is open, the sidebar drags down its edge by the handle above it, and Ctrl+wheel over it sizes the buttons; right-click a button to show that section - the sidebar, the Actions window or the menus - as **Words**, **Icons** or **Icon and word**; Escape leaves.

Buttons can show their word, an icon, or both: **View › Interface › Icons** sets it for the sidebar, the Actions window and the menus, each on its own (words on the sidebar, both elsewhere, to start with); a verb with no icon keeps its word. The icons are the Tabler set (MIT licence, `Resources/Icons/Tabler/LICENSE.txt`).

Every verb also lives in a menu on the top strip: **Create · Geometry · Shape · Surface · UV · Select · View**, with the key in grey at the right. **View** also holds four submenus - **Show** (the ticks of the View tab), **Shading** (Matcap, its looks, the wires), **Windows** and **Interface**. Every window is a verb too - Actions, Assets, Material Library, Material Manager, Textures, UVs, Side Panel (Paint and Flags are SidePanel tabs; Settings and Edit UI are verbs of the interface) - so any of them can go on the sidebar or in the favourites; the windows live under View › Windows and nowhere else in the menus. The viewport's right-click keeps Windows and Interface as lists; with the mode off, the toolbar button enters it and the editor's own Window menu lists the tabs.

The **SidePanel** (**N**, or the **Side Panel** verb; open from the start) stands down the edge the bar is not on, its tabs down the outside: **Item** - the location, rotation and scale of what is picked, and the pivot; a picked group says **Group of 6** with **Ungroup** beside it; with nothing picked, the level itself: its name and its **Bakes itself** tick; **Paint** - the brush; **Flags** - what the bake does with the picked pieces; **View** - three lines, what is reached for often on its chip and the rest under the small arrow at the line's end: **View** (Lit and Unlit with the editor's own icons, Matcap - its clay ball, the arrow beside it holding the clay's look and colour - and Paint, its green-to-red ball); **Wires** (Edited, All, X-ray; the arrow: Through, IDs, the colours - edited, others, primitives - and their **strength**: under 1 they fade, what is picked stays full); **Show** (Overlay, Grid, Names, Gizmo; the arrow: Origins, Prefab bounds, Group bounds, Hints, the grid's Axes and strength, the backdrop's gradient and colours, and the look round an open prefab). A fourth line, **Context**, stands only while a prefab is open where it stands: **Ghost**, **Wire** - its wires only - or **Regular**, as it is in the view in use; out of reach whichever. **Assets** - prefabs, static meshes and levels as tiles: a search (`p:` / `sm:` / `li:` narrow to a kind), the P / SM / LI ticks, a folder picked in a tree of the project's folders, −/+ or Ctrl+wheel for the tile size - all the way down is a list. Names drop their SM_ / PF_ / LI_ in front (the full name is the tooltip). Double-click a tile or drag it into the viewport to place it where the view looks (a mesh or a level dropped from the content browser counts too); a placed mesh is the editor's own StaticMesh actor and a placed level its Level Instance actor, tagged as ours, in the outliner's SurfacingPro › Assets drawer, with the same origin mark as a placed prefab - inside an open prefab a mesh or a level becomes a part, carried by every instance (a level as a level instance of its own inside each). The open level itself is not offered, and a level that cannot be an instance here says so in the readout. Shift-click adds a placed mesh, prefab or level to the selection, Ctrl-click takes one out (a click on anything inside a placed level takes the level); in 4 out in the level, a box or a lasso over placed things with none of our pieces in it picks them (Shift adds, Ctrl takes out); G R S move them together - G snaps their origin and box corners to our points, the surface under the cursor and what is in reach, as a primitive does - and R pressed again turns them as a ball under the cursor. Names shows their labels as it shows the prefabs'. **Materials** - the Material Library in the panel: the same menus, search and list (the picked materials blue, a click puts one on the selection, right-click for the Material Manager); the window keeps the surfacing info. Every window's tab wears the icon of its verb. Right-click a tile: **Place** (at the middle of what is picked - our geometry, else the picked actors - or at the origin with nothing picked), **Replace the picked with this** (prefab, mesh or level instance for any other, where each stands), for a prefab **Open**, **Find in the level**, **Painted meshes**, **Save** and **Delete**, then **Open in the asset editor**, **Show in the content browser**; and for what is picked in the level - here and on the viewport's right-click - **Replace the picked with...** (a picker with a search over prefabs, meshes and levels), **Details** (the editor's panel in a window) and **Materials** (each slot: pick a material, or the mesh's own back). Click the open tab to fold the panel to its tabs; **Ctrl+click** pins a tab beside the others, so several stand stacked; drag a tab up or down to reorder. Drag the inner edge to size it; it stands as tall as what is shown. Press **X** over the viewport and type to find any verb or window by name; Enter runs the first match. The pane under the cursor takes the keys - the viewport or the UVs window, whichever the hand is over. A verb that is a switch says its state (Grid: on). The settings come last, each with its section in grey: a click or Enter flips a tick or steps a choice and the search stays open; a number is a field with up and down arrows, or type it after the name ("point size 6"). The **settings** tick beside the field turns that off. A verb's frame of numbers (after Bevel, Extrude, Solidify...) moves by the short bar along its top; the options in it are the ones its numbers do not already hold.

**Q** opens your favourites at the cursor. Star a verb in its parameter frame (the ★ at the foot) and it is there.

The **foot line** under the viewport is one grey line: what is on (the layer, the density readout), then the keys that mean something right now - `G move · E extrude · Ctrl+R loop …` - or, during a verb, what it is waiting for. A refusal shows amber for a few seconds. Interface › **Hints** turns it off.

Every verb with numbers or options opens a small **frame** at the corner of the viewport when it runs: its knobs and options (drag a knob or tick an option and the verb is undone and run again, live, as long as it was the last thing done), its star. A left click in the viewport puts it away; right-click any button or menu row › **Options...** to open it without running the verb.

The mode keeps a **session record** - keys, verbs, selections and your notes, no geometry - under `Saved/SurfacingPro/Sessions/` in the project. Type a line in X that matches nothing and press Enter to leave a note in it. **Record sessions** in the settings turns it off.

## 3. Making something

**Primitive** puts the last kind on what is under the cursor (standing on it), or at the world origin when the cursor is over nothing - on the grid, and clear of anything already there unless it was aimed - and opens its frame for the kind - **Box, Cylinder, Cone, Tube, Bevel, Endcap** - and its size. **Patch** alone makes a patch (its size in its frame); with faces picked it lays a patch on each of them, already surfaced; its frame also puts one in place of a whole primitive. E on a side of a primitive stands a new piece on it and pulls it straight out along the side (X Y Z send it along an axis instead). With every side of a primitive picked, Extrude grows the primitive; a primitive never loses a side - Cut takes a part off, and Delete takes it whole (4, or A in 3). With a primitive's face picked, Cone, Tube, Bevel and Endcap replace the primitive with a curved patch filling the same space, already surfaced. A primitive's corners show only while it is being worked on (a side or a corner of it picked); **A** on it takes every corner in 1, every side otherwise.

**Cut** (K) cuts primitives with a plane: click two points for a vertical cut, three for any plane; the cut is drawn across every primitive it would split; Cut again or Enter cuts. Its frame chooses both halves, the front or the back. With a patch line picked, Cut splits the patch there instead. There is no CSG: a concave shape is several convex primitives, which is why a primitive can never come out broken.

New geometry made with nothing selected goes into a **group of its own**; made with something of yours selected, it joins that group. Everything of ours stands under the **SurfacingPro** folder in the outliner.

## 4. Selecting

**1 2 3 4** say what a click picks: a **point**, an **edge**, a **face**, or the whole **thing** (a patch, a primitive, a placed prefab). The mode opens in 4, Object; **Tab** goes to the Edit kind last used and back. A click picks what is under the cursor, nearest first; the same spot clicked again steps to the piece behind it, and a click anywhere else starts at the front again.

| Sub-mode | A click takes | With Alt | With Shift / Ctrl |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Points | one control point | the row; again, the column | adds; Ctrl-click a second point takes everything between |
| 2 Edges | one edge | the whole line | adds; Ctrl-click takes everything between |
| 3 Faces | one cell (a primitive face when no cell is in the way) | a row of faces | adds; Ctrl-click takes the block between |
| 4 Things | the whole patch or primitive | — | Shift adds, Ctrl takes out |

On a primitive, Alt takes a loop too: in 2, the rim of a cap (a cylinder's ring) or every parallel edge; in 3, every face turning across the edge nearest the click; Shift adds. **A** selects all of what you are in - in 4, every patch and primitive; **Ctrl+L** (Linked) adds every patch joined to the picked ones through a shared point, and on; **Alt+A** none, **Ctrl+I** inverts (in 3, the faces). **Ctrl + numpad +** / **-** grows or shrinks the selection by a ring (Grow / Shrink in the Select menu). Going from 1 to 3 turns picked points into their faces; 3 to 1 keeps the corners; 4 starts over. In 2, two clicks on a primitive pick one of its edges. A bare drag is a **box** select (Shift extends, Ctrl subtracts); **B** arms one, **L** a lasso, **W** cycles to the brush whose size is the wheel. **Same** (Select menu) adds everything wearing the active material; **Every Other** takes every second line.

**Groups.** **Group** (Ctrl+G, the Select menu, Q, the right-click) makes the picked pieces - patches and primitives together - one group: in 4 a click on any of them picks them all, Shift adds a whole group, Ctrl takes one out, and a box or lasso touching one takes the group. **Alt+click** picks one piece alone; Alt+click it again for the group round it, again for the one round that - groups nest: group two groups, or a group and a loose piece, and they go inside the new one; Alt-pick pieces inside a group and Group them for a group inside it. A picked group is bracketed at its corners with **Group of 6** over it, and the Item tab says the same; **Group bounds** (View › Show ▾, off at first) brackets every group faintly, and **Names** puts the plate over every group. G R S, the gizmo, Duplicate (the copy is a group of its own) and Delete take the whole group, primitives with the patches. **Ungroup** (Ctrl+Alt+G, or the Item tab) takes one level off the picked group; on a piece picked alone, it takes the piece out of its group. Tab or 1 2 3 on a group edits its patches together.

**Statistics** (a View verb) puts a small grey line in the viewport's bottom-right corner: patches, primitives, points, triangles and materials of what is picked, else of the whole level with the prefabs placed.

**Z** walks the wire views: the patches being edited · every patch · every patch x-ray - every patch meaning placed prefabs' too. **Alt+Z** (or the **Overlay** verb) hides every wire of ours, the selection's too, and brings them back. **Shift+Z** switches between the modelling view and the shaded one. The modelling view has a quiet gradient behind it; the editor's own **Grid** (eye menu) draws, while the mode is up, a metric floor at zero with the X (red) and Y (green) axes through the origin; **Matcap** puts clay on every patch and primitive (five looks under the arrow beside Matcap in the SidePanel's View tab, each with its ball: clay, soft grey, terracotta, studio, red wax; a pick turns the matcap view on) - the backdrop in the SidePanel's View tab, **Matcap** as a view mode of its own, in the SurfacingPro section at the top of the view-mode menu (with **Cage**: the three wire views and the surface wireframe); **Matcap Look** beside it picks the ball; Unlit is ticked only without the clay: unlit underneath, clay from a studio grey sphere on everything, tinted by its colour; Lit or any other mode turns it off; Game view (G) goes to Lit and G again back to what it was; any edit of ours made while it is on turns it back to the editor's view first (picking Unlit or Matcap leaves Game view), all three in the SidePanel's View tab and the settings. In the editor's own wireframe view, clicks select through.

**H** hides the selection - patches and primitives - **Shift+H** the rest, **Alt+H** shows everything.

Undo hands back the selection that was there when the edit was made. An extrude (E) or a duplicate (Shift+D) and the move, turn or size that pulled it out are one step: one undo takes both back, one redo brings both back.

A click in Edge mode takes the line nearest the cursor within 40 px (Settings › Display › Edge Pick Radius); on a curved primitive the surface's own edge counts as well as the straight net line. A patch left with no surface - a line of points - is dropped when its prefab or level loads.

## 5. Moving and shaping

**G** moves, **R** turns, **S** sizes - points, a patch, a primitive, a placed prefab: whatever is picked. **G** with nothing picked switches Game view, as the Game chip does. **X**, **Y** or **Z** locks the axis (again: the plane; again: free) and the piece then follows the mouse along it; the same key again (G G, R R, S S) frees the lock - and G G with nothing locked slides the points along the surface, a third G moving them again; type a number for an exact amount; left click or Enter keeps it, right click or Escape drops it. The mouse never runs out of screen: past an edge it comes back in at the other, a locked move included - past the horizon a plane move holds where it was. In 3 on a primitive, G pushes the picked faces along their own normals. The **gizmo** does the same by hand - arrows, plane squares, rings, and a ball while turning; off in the settings.

The SidePanel's **Item** tab holds the numbers: one line each - Location, Rotation, Scale, Origin (on a patch or primitive the middle two read Turn and Size, dimmed: they have no rotation or scale of their own, a number there applies and reads 0 or 1 again); type or drag (a drag applies as it goes, one undo step): a location takes the selection there, a rotation or a scale is applied - ours then read 0 and 1 again, an actor keeps its own. A faint cross beside a number puts it back (0, or 1 for a size), one at the end of a line the whole line, **× all** everything and the origin. A patch wearing a see-through material also gets a **Layer** line, 0 to 4: 0 the opaque surface, each level drawn over the one below. **UVs follow** (Item tab, and the settings) off keeps the texture where it was when points move. Right-click a row to copy or paste three numbers (UE's Details copies paste too).

**Origin**: right-click in the viewport › **Set Origin to Selection** sets it on what is picked - one vertex exactly, faces or several vertices at their centre, a patch or a primitive (an actor or a placed prefab keeps its own) - and an orange ring marks it. Rotation and scale then go about it - the gizmo, R, S and the Item numbers; moves still go from the centre. **Origin back to the centre** (same menu, or Centre in Item) clears it; leaving the mode forgets it.

**Snapping**: the snap chip on the top line (the magnet) turns snapping on and off; resting the cursor on the small arrow beside it opens the snap menu - the switch (**Shift+Tab**) and what it snaps to (**Vertex**, **Edge**, **Edge centre**, **Edge perpendicular** - the square foot from where the point started - **Face**, **Increment**) on top; a green ring marks a target of ours (a cross in it for a middle, a corner for a square foot), orange the level's meshes; **Snap with** (which point of what moves lands: Closest, Centre of its box, Median, or the Active - last picked - point), **Targets** (level meshes, own piece, axis guides, **Stand on the face** - a placed mesh, prefab or level that lands on a surface under G turns to stand on it, its up along the surface - dropped assets), **Reach**, **Increment size**, **Grid step** and **Move, rotate, scale steps** (the editor's own, with their sizes) as submenus. Hold **Ctrl** during a move to flip snapping for as long as it is held (the editor's steps too). While you move, on by default: the control point or primitive corner under the cursor takes the moving point nearest the cursor; **Surfaces** on the snap chip lands on whatever is under the cursor; otherwise anything within reach lands point for point. With nothing in reach, **Align with far points** lands the moving point on the X, Y or Z line through any other point that passes within reach - a dotted guide in the axis colour says which; with an axis locked, a point anywhere along that axis is a target. The two digits on the top line are the reaches, 0-9: **wheel** during a move sizes the soft reach, **Ctrl+wheel** the snap reach (each shows its ring for a moment); **[ ]** and **Shift+[ ]** step them any time. The arrow beside either chip has the centimetres and the options; right-click either chip for them too.

**Soft selection** (**O**, or a click on the rings chip on the top line) lets unselected points follow the selected ones by distance - straight through space, or **Along the grid** (the soft chip's menu, which also holds the reach as a number and each falloff with its curve drawn) by walking the net; **Shift+O** cycles the falloff. The soft ring is drawn in every kind but 4.

**Continue**: moving a patch's border towards another patch's border draws a green ghost where it would join; keep the move and the two are one surface. Continue on the shelf does the same from a selected border. Its frame turns the offer off.

The verbs - on the bar, the shelf, the menus and X:

| Verb | Key | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Extrude | E | Grows the patch by a line past the selected border and moves it (on a patch whose UVs are held, the frame's **UVs carry on** keeps the texture going by the new line's length); on faces, a lid and its walls (what happens beneath is in the frame). An interior line with the frame's tick cuts the patch there and grows the near half. |
| Loop | Ctrl+R | A line through the patch where the cursor is, carried on into the patches joined to it along the way (shown before the click); the wheel adds more; click, slide, click - the carried lines slide with it. Row or column in the frame. |
| Push | | The picked faces one grid step along their normals; in or out. |
| Steps | | Several extrudes at once from two edges, or two whole lines side by side: count and rise in the frame. |
| Bevel | Ctrl+B | Rounds the crease along the picked lines; width and segments in the frame; it eats the lines it reaches. |
| Subdivide | | A line between every pair of lines, each way as far as it fits. |
| Lattice | | A cage of points round the selected patches, its points each way (X, Y, Z) in its frame. Click the cage's points (Shift adds, A all), G R S move them and the patch bends smoothly with them; Escape leaves the cage, Delete removes it and the shape stays; Lattice again goes back in. |
| Weld | M | Collapses the picked points onto their centre (or the first picked, or by distance). Across patches. |
| Join | | One patch of the picked patches wherever two share an edge. |
| Bridge | | Two opposite borders of one patch close it into a tube: one line more, lying on the first. Otherwise a patch between two picked lines of the same length; two loops make a tube. |
| Separate | P | The picked block of faces out as a patch of its own. |
| Duplicate | Shift+D | Copies the selection - patches, faces, primitives, actors - and moves it. Ctrl+C / X / V through a clipboard. |
| Delete | Delete | The thing, whole lines, or faces. |
| Solidify | | Thickness: front, back and skirts. |
| Smooth · Inflate · Flatten · Random | | Relax; push along the normal; onto a plane (X, Y, Z ticks in the frame); jitter by up to N cm (Shift+R rolls again). |
| Normals | | A click smooths the picked patches and primitives, again makes them hard; right-click: smooth by angle, and the angle. A new cylinder is round at 60°, a box hard. |
| Crease | | The picked whole lines (Alt-click in 1) shade hard whatever the shading; again to smooth them. |
| Flip · Face Outward | · Shift+N | Inside out; every patch facing away from the middle. |
| Apply | Ctrl+Shift+Enter | A curve's surface baked into an editable grid. |

**Shift+R** runs the last verb again on the new selection.

## 6. Surfacing

Every patch and primitive face wears one material. **Material Library** opens the library: a click puts the material on the selection; its **Material** menu makes instances (saved in `SurfacingPro/MaterialInstances`, beside `Materials`, where the parents are; **Tidy instance folders** moves instances left in the older `MI` and `Materials/Instances` folders there) and copies and opens the Material Manager and Textures, its **View** menu holds Pictures and Small rows. The materials the selection wears - our geometry's, else the picked meshes' - are blue in the list, the first selected, as the selection changes (a material outside the library is not in the list). Right-click a row: **New instance beside**, **Copy**, **Open in the asset editor**, **Open in the Material Manager**, **Render pictures again**. **Material Manager** (its **Material** menu: New, Duplicate, Rename, Delete, Apply, Save, Revert, Export and Import as files; its **View** menu: pictures, order, shape) shows the selection's material on a preview cube with its parameters beside it to edit and save; it follows the selection when the selection changes (a row picked in it stays until then). The viewport's right-click opens it on what the picked wears: **Open M_Brick in the Material Manager**, or with several, a list to choose from. **Textures** is one window in two halves - any folder on this machine on the left, the library on the right; **Files** / **Library** on its top line fold either away - and brings image files in as the project's own textures. Each half has one line: the folder, **Up**, **Pick files...**, **Import** and **More** (Samples, In Explorer, Favourite, how the files come in - conform or linked - the filters, the order); the library's count and its **More** (Conform the picked, Conform all). The Material Manager's right-click writes the shown instance out as a small file (**Export to file**, `SurfacingPro/Library/Materials/*.material.json`, described in `Docs/LibraryFiles.md`) and reads such files back (**Import from file...**). The **UVs** window keeps the net large: **Actions** (the UV verbs) along its top with a dim word at the right when nothing is picked or a patch is frozen, and a quickpanel down its right, the SidePanel's manner (**N** over the window opens and closes it; a tab's click shows it alone, again folds the panel to its tabs, Ctrl+click pins one beside the others) - **Select**, **Transform**, **Islands**, **Mapping** (metres a tile across and down, Square, the shift, the turn and a quarter turn, the flips, Smooth) and **View** (the active patch's texture - or the checker it wears - faint behind the nets, on the first tile or repeated on every tile).

A material is placed by projection - two axes and a scale in metres per tile. The verbs:

| Verb | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Flow** | A metre of surface is a metre of texture wherever it bends. For anything curved. |
| **Even** | Every cell the same size in the texture, whatever its size in the world. For trims laid by the cell. |
| **Align** | Flat on each surface's own plane. |
| **Project** | One projection for everything selected, along an axis or from the camera (its right-click), so a bent wall has no seam. |
| **Fit** | A whole number of tiles (or trim cells) across each surface. |
| **Density** | Every selected surface to the same texels a metre as the active one. |
| **Freeze** | Holds a patch's UVs where they are. |

Their frames hold the knobs: metres a tile across and down, the turn, flips, a quarter turn, swap, and **Inherit** - take the last clicked face's projection, the gesture that closes a seam. Knobs are relative by default; tick **match** for every surface to take the numbers outright. The gesture: click the surface that is right, then the ones that should match it, then the verb.

**Middle-click** a surface to copy its whole surface onto the selection; **Shift + middle-click** the other way - the selection's material onto the surface under the cursor - and **Ctrl+Shift + middle-click** its mapping too. **Replace** swaps one material for another across the level with every projection kept. The **texels a metre** readout on the foot line turns amber when the selection disagrees with itself.

The **UV window** (Windows › UVs) draws the selected patches' nets over the tile grid, live. Click, Shift-click (adds), Ctrl-click (takes out), box (Shift adds, Ctrl takes out), A pick points; a click on a line between two points picks both, **Alt** on a line its whole row or column, Alt on a point cycles row, column, point as in the viewport, and a **double-click** on a net takes the net whole (Shift adds) - the viewport shows the same picks as they happen; **G R S** (with the cursor over the window - the keys follow it) move, turn and size them with X and Y locks and typed numbers; **Ctrl+R** turns a quarter. After an R or S a small frame at the view's foot keeps its numbers: type the exact figure, or for a scale press **½, ×2, 1:1**; the cross puts it away. A move's numbers are the foot line's U and V. The quickpanel floats over the window's right edge, the nets running on under it (**N** over the window, or the chevron at the top line's right; tabs as the SidePanel's - click one for it alone, again to fold, Ctrl+click to pin, drag up or down to reorder; its inner edge drags it wider) holds **Select** - All, None, Invert, Linked, Border (the outer ring of points of each selected patch) - **Transform** - Align U / V to the selection's low edge, centre or high edge, Mirror across or down about the selection's centre, ¼ turn; a patch with a rule is frozen first, and the line says so - **Islands** - **Pack** lays the selected patches' nets into the 0,0 tile, tallest first, their sizes kept relative and the whole scaled to fill, the gap set by the margin beside it; **Stack** moves every selected net onto the first's centre, for alike pieces that should share one place on the texture; **Rectify** makes each selected net an even grid inside its own box, its turn kept; **Straighten** puts the picked points of one row or column on one line along the axis they lean to (all four are UV verbs too, in Actions and the top strip's UV menu) - **Mapping** - the mapping's numbers, metres a tile, Square, the shift, the turn, the flips, Smooth, Even, set outright on every selected patch - and **View**. The foot line holds **U** and **V** - the middle of what is picked (the picked points, faces or edges, else the selected nets): type a number, click the small arrows - or hold one and drag up or down, the nets following - or roll the wheel over the field (Shift: ten times the step, Ctrl: a tenth) and the selection moves at once; the crosshair beside puts the selection's low corner on 0,0 (the 0,0 tile is framed in blue and named); the selection's box size sits in grey after. Every number in the window is such a field. Picked points on a patch whose mapping is a rule freeze the patch first and move alone - the readout says "(1 frozen)"; with nothing picked, a rule slides whole. F frames the nets in the part of the window the panel leaves. **X** over the window searches the UV verbs and the surface settings only. With the **Snap** switch on, the point in hand (or the selection's middle under G) lands on an unpicked point of the nets within reach, else on the tile grid's lines - whole tiles, quarters when they are drawn. With **Soft selection** on (O), the unpicked points of a frozen net follow the picked ones by their distance across the net, the reach and falloff of the soft chip. The View tab's **Stretch** tick colours every cell by how its share of the texture compares with its share of the surface, against the net's own middle - blue tight, green even, red stretched - to see where a net was pulled about.

## 7. Layers and painting

A layer is a patch on top of another, lifted a hair so the order is right - decals and blends. **Layer** moves the selection to the next layer, 0 to 3 and round; the foot line reads it. **Copy Up** copies the selection one layer up in place.

**Paint** (Shift+B) paints the vertex colour on the ticked channels: a click on R, G, B or A in the Paint tab takes that one, a Shift-click adds or takes one, and the brush, Fill, Clear and the adjustments write them all. **R, G and B ticked together are the colour**: a drag paints the first swatch, a Ctrl-drag the second; any other set goes up with a drag and down with a Ctrl-drag, and A rides beside the colour when it is ticked too. Drag paints up, Ctrl-drag down, Shift-drag smooths, Ctrl+wheel sizes the brush, Escape puts it away; the gizmo is away while the brush is out. The SidePanel's **Paint** tab is one compact sheet: the brush (with nothing picked it paints whatever it reaches; with a selection, that alone), **View** (the Paint view, on or off), the channels, the two colours - white and black to start; picking one ticks R, G and B - (a drag paints the first, a Ctrl-drag the second; ⇄ or **Shift+X** in the viewport swaps them, brush out or not), strength and size as fields, Fill, Clear, and the adjustments below. The **Paint** view - the ticked channel on every surface, black where empty, the channel's own colour where full, as painted: a full channel is pure red, green or blue on the screen and a half is half (the view is drawn without the editor's tonemapper while it shows); R, G and B together, or several, show the colour - is a view kind beside Lit, Unlit and Matcap (the view-mode menu's SurfacingPro section, the View tab, the Paint View verb); the brush out shows it on its own. Placed meshes wear it too - every slot, their own materials back when it goes. Where nothing is painted the view is black. The brush on a mesh paints that placed mesh alone - the mesh asset is untouched, two copies paint apart. A mesh inside a prefab takes the brush only when the prefab's **Painted meshes** is on (right-click its tile in the Assets tab, or the asset's details): the paint is then kept with the part and every instance wears it; off, a mesh inside is not paintable and the readout says so. One stroke is one undo step.

**Adjust** (the Paint tab; verbs too, in Surface): five presses on the tab's channels of the picked points - every point of the selected pieces when none is picked - and of the picked meshes, each with a frame afterwards whose knobs run it again live, as every verb's does. **From Cavity** writes the dirt in - nothing on the flat, full in a crevice; patches that meet are read as one surface, so the line where a floor meets a wall is a crevice - through a blend (Replace, Add, Subtract, Multiply, Lighten, Darken) by an amount, with the cavity's Smooth (0 none, 0.5 one ring, 1 two), Level (the gain: 4 fills a shallow crevice already) and Invert (the ridges instead - wear on the edges): dirt in every crevice in one press. **Smooth** eases the channels towards their neighbours - across patches that meet, as From Cavity reads them - by an amount, in passes. **Contrast** pushes them from the middle (0 as they are, 1 twice as far, -1 flat). **Levels** remaps them as a picture editor's levels do: in black and white, gamma, out black and white. **Invert** turns them over. A placed static mesh takes the brush, Fill, Clear and the adjustments too: the colours go into its own per-instance vertex colours (LOD 0), as the editor's mesh paint writes them, so two copies can be painted differently and a reimport keeps the paint. There is no mask for now.

## 8. Prefabs and levels

A **prefab** is one asset of our geometry placed by instances; edit it where it stands and every instance follows. A **level** is a level of its own that can hold anything - lights, blueprints, meshes, our groups, prefabs - placed where it stood. Use a prefab for a door frame and a level for the room it is in.

**Prefab** and **Level** (Create) make one from the selection - patches, primitives, meshes, lights, anything - named by you, and leave it placed, selected. Both are saved under `/Game/SurfacingPro/` (Prefabs, Levels). A level made elsewhere in the project is listed all the same.

**Open** (Alt+PgDn, Enter, or a double-click) opens the selected one: alone at the origin, or where it stands with the level round it - ghosted, as wires, or as it is, the View tab's **Context** (Open's frame). Whatever you make while it is open belongs to it - our geometry, and a mesh or a light dragged in. **Close** (Alt+PgUp) writes it back and every instance follows; Close's frame drops the edits instead. A double-click on empty sky closes too; in the level itself it leaves the SurfacingPro mode. **Unpack** turns an instance back into plain geometry where it stood; **Unique** makes the selected instance its own copy.

In the outliner the level's own geometry is a group named after the level, under the **SurfacingPro** folder, and the prefabs, meshes and levels placed in the level stand in its **Assets** drawer - one folder to open, sort and select from. The bake's own meshes are not listed in the outliner: they are made and remade by Bake, and an edit brings the previews back. **Game view** (the Game chip, or the editor's own menu; G with nothing picked no longer opens it) shows the game alone, lit - none of our wires, boxes, marks or names; the sidebar and panels stay, so the mode does not leave. **Overlay** (Alt+Z, X search, or the View family) draws the editing overlay over the game for this look; entering Game view again hides it again.

A placed prefab's flags: pick it and open the SidePanel's **Flags** tab - **Override** gives the whole placement its own Detail / No collision / No shadow / No draw, over every piece in it, for its bake; **Cancel override** goes back. Beside it, **Paint override** lets the brush paint that placed prefab where it stands: it starts from the prefab's paint, keeps its own (the asset and the other instances keep theirs) and bakes as a mesh of its own; **Cancel paint override** drops it and shows the prefab's paint again. With the brush out, a placed prefab without it is not painted - the cursor turns to a small cross over it; open the prefab (Alt+PgDn) to paint the asset itself. To differ piece by piece, save it as a new prefab or unpack it.

A placed prefab shows its origin cross and its box, faintly; the eye menu's **Prefab Origins** and **Prefab Bounds** (and the settings) turn each off on its own.

The library is the SidePanel's **Assets** tab (prefabs, meshes and levels as tiles): double-click or drag to place; right-click a prefab for **Edit** (open it), **Find in the level** (select its instances), **Painted meshes**, Save, Delete, Replace the picked with this, Open in its editor, Show in Content Browser. A tile's tooltip says the prefab's pieces, the parts it carries, how many are placed, and whether it is unsaved. Every prefab shows its own picture in the Content Browser.

Inside an open prefab, **Prefab** places rather than spawns, so a prefab can hold others; a placed one moves with G R S, opens with a double-click, and Delete takes it out. A level placed or dropped inside an open prefab is a part of it the same way - a container inside a container - and comes back as a level instance wherever the prefab stands.

## 9. Playing and shipping

There is no bake to think about. Switching to Game view, pressing Play, saving, or keeping a level turns what you built into merged Nanite static meshes - one per group and 20 m cell, a section per material, collision from the surface - and any edit brings the editable geometry back. The SidePanel's **Flags** tab sets what the pieces do when they ship: detail, no collision, no shadow, no draw. Create › **Bake** forces it by hand; the level's own **Bakes itself** tick is in the Item tab with nothing picked.

## 10. Settings and keys

**Settings** (a verb in View) opens the plugin's settings on the editor's own preferences page (Edit › Editor Preferences › Plugins › SurfacingPro); the common knobs also sit in the verbs' frames, and the button arrangements under Interface. Backspace over a knob puts its default back. The grid step, snapping, soft selection, the gizmo, the wire views, the new-piece sizes, the bake cell size, the materials library root - all there, in twelve headings, saved per user. The brush's channel and colours and the frames' knobs are not on the page: the Paint tab and the frames are where they live.

## 11. Words

**Group** — pieces (patches and primitives) picked and moved as one; groups nest. **Container** — the actor that holds your geometry; you rarely see it. **Primitive** — a convex solid made of planes; its **faces** are surfaced one by one. **Patch** — a grid of control points with a bezier surface through them. **Cell** — one square of a patch's grid. **Tile** — one repeat of a material; scale is metres per tile. **Layer** — a patch's place in the stack, 0 to 3. **Prefab** — an asset of our geometry placed by instances; it carries its own icon, and its name is yours (no PF_ put in front). **Level** — a level placed inside another.
